Overview

Saturdays is a consumer food-ordering marketplace I built and shipped as the sole technical lead within the LMX Labs product ecosystem — owning it from information architecture through to live, money-moving checkout.

It is not a demo. It runs in production on its own domain, takes real orders, and settles real payments through two independent gateways. The engineering priority throughout was the reliability of the ordering-to-payment path, because that is the only part of a food-delivery product a customer cannot forgive getting wrong.

What was built

Storefront & menu discovery

A browsable catalogue with category navigation and item detail, built as the customer's entry point and tuned for fast, mobile-first discovery.

Cart & checkout

A stateful cart and a guided checkout that holds the order together from selection to payment, with validation at each step so a customer never reaches payment with an inconsistent order.

Dual payment integration

Both Stripe and PhonePe wired in as first-class options, covering card and India-native UPI flows so checkout meets customers on the rails they actually use.

Order tracking & real-time sync

Orders sync to the DineGuru restaurant-operations backend over REST APIs and webhooks, so a placed order becomes a live kitchen ticket without a human re-keying it.

How it was built

Component system

A React + TypeScript front end assembled on MUI and Radix UI primitives — accessible, composable building blocks — so the interface stayed consistent and keyboard-navigable as surface area grew.

Payments as the critical path

Stripe's React SDK and PhonePe's flow were integrated behind a unified checkout, treating the payment step as the system's most failure-sensitive path and handling its states deliberately rather than optimistically.

Cross-product integration

REST APIs and webhooks bind Saturdays to DineGuru for real-time order sync — the storefront and the restaurant's operations run as one system, not two apps that happen to share data.

Shipped on Vercel

Deployed and iterated on Vercel, keeping the path from commit to live short enough to fix and improve a product that was already serving customers.

Engineering challenges

Two gateways, one checkout

Stripe and PhonePe have different flows, states and failure modes. The work was in presenting them as one coherent checkout to the customer while keeping their handling correct and separate underneath.

Keeping two products in sync

An order placed on Saturdays has to appear reliably as a ticket in DineGuru. Getting that sync trustworthy over webhooks — not just working on the happy path — was the difference between a storefront and an operations platform.
